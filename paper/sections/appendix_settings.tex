\subsection{Tail Growth Ratio Sensitivity}
\label{app:shape}
Fixing $H = 12$ and $B = 20$ on CogVideoX-2B (17 frames, 32 prompts) and varying only the geometric tail growth ratio $r \in \{1.0, 1.4, 2.0, 3.0\}$ yields $0.1656$ LPIPS ($0.8958$ SSIM, $27.44$~dB PSNR) for a uniform tail ($r=1.0$), compared to $0.1560$ ($28.61$~dB), $0.1566$ ($28.32$~dB), and $\mathbf{0.1541}$ ($29.08$~dB) at $r = 1.4, 2.0, 3.0$. Growing tail gaps improve LPIPS by $5\text{--}7\%$ over uniform tail spacing and exhibit broad insensitivity across $r \in [1.4, 3.0]$.

\caption{\textbf{Head-length ablation} on CogVideoX-2B (17 frames, 32 prompts). Under-allocating the head ($H=6$) nearly doubles perceptual error; at 49 frames and 100 prompts, $H=16$ ($0.1300$ LPIPS, $31.63$~dB) likewise beats $H=12$ ($0.1424$ LPIPS, $30.21$~dB), validating Theorem~\ref{thm:kkt}.}

Table~\ref{tab:head} empirically confirms the KKT head-saturation law: short prefixes ($H \le 6$, and a fortiori $M=3$ or uniform $H=0$) admit large early errors where $\norm{\Psi_{j\to T}}$ is maximal, whereas expanding $H$ in accordance with Bellman DP optimality ($H^\star(22) = 16$) yields substantial perceptual gains across both 17-frame and 49-frame generation.
