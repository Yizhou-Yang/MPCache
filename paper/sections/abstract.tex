Video diffusion transformers spend most of their inference budget on repeated denoiser calls, and classifier-free guidance doubles that budget by running a conditional and an unconditional pass at every step. Caching recovers part of it by replacing some calls with reused or predicted values, but the replacement and the decision to make it are designed separately, so an inexpensive local approximation can accumulate into large terminal error. We formulate training-free caching as a receding-horizon control problem and introduce \method, which fits a structured local model of the guided denoising field online from the exact evaluations already computed along the trajectory, using standard tools from system identification and control rather than new analysis. This shared model couples three operations without extra network probes: state-conditioned forecasting predicts skipped outputs from latent innovations, solver-aware pricing propagates step errors through the sampling dynamics to the terminal step, and pre-update residual checks adjust the model's trust region at each exact evaluation. A budgeted planner then allocates remaining exact evaluations across the horizon while leaving model weights and sampling grids unchanged. On CogVideoX-2B, Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, and HunyuanVideo, we establish a unified evaluation harness across TeaCache, TaylorSeer, and RA-CFGCache and use component-level diagnostic gates to characterize when online local identification improves upon offline propagation priors.
 The original source code, including the full experimental trajectories and results, is provided at \url{https://github.com/Yizhou-Yang/MPCache}.
